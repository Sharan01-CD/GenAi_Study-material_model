ABSTRACT

The rapid acceleration of generative artificial intelligence and large language models (LLMs) provides unprecedented opportunities to revolutionize personalized education, active recall, and continuous diagnostic self-assessment. Traditional study approaches often suffer from passive reading, lack of immediate feedback, and disconnected assessment tools.

This project presents CogniLearn, an autonomous, end-to-end generative AI educational platform capable of transforming raw academic text and multi-page PDF documents into comprehensive pedagogical study materials and rigorous diagnostic evaluations. Powered by Google's state-of-the-art Gemini Flash multimodal model via the @google/genai TypeScript SDK and Python SDK, the platform implements strict JSON schema enforcement to produce structured curriculum outputs, including:

  • Executive summaries with foundational mental models and learning objectives;
  • Modular curriculum breakdowns with Feynman-style analogies, real-world case studies, and common pitfall warnings;
  • High-yield cheat sheets featuring crucial definitions, formulas, and mnemonics;
  • 3D interactive flashcards optimized for active recall;
  • Multi-format diagnostic question suites comprising multiple-choice questions (MCQs), clinical/applied scenario cases, true/false conceptual checks, and open-ended short answers with real-time automated rubric scoring.

To support longitudinal retention, CogniLearn incorporates a Smart Study Scheduler based on Ebbinghaus' forgetting curve, actively tracking study intervals and issuing automated alerts when a deck exceeds 24 hours without review. The system is engineered as a dual-architecture deployment: a high-performance full-stack TypeScript/React SPA running on Express and Vite, alongside a native Python Streamlit application designed for rapid cloud deployment. Latency optimization techniques—such as parallelized asynchronous generation, prompt minimization, and transient error backoff mechanisms—reduce generation latency to under 5 seconds for focused question suites.

Keywords: Generative AI, Large Language Models, Gemini Flash, Automated Question Generation, Active Recall, Spaced Repetition, Streamlit, React, TypeScript.

PROJECT AIM: The objective of this project is to architect, develop, and benchmark an automated generative AI platform that converts arbitrary academic documents into structured pedagogical learning materials, interactive flashcards, and diagnostic question banks with real-time AI-guided scoring and spaced review scheduling.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background and Context

In higher education and technical certifications, students and professionals are confronted with vast volumes of textual information—dense research papers, complex textbook chapters, and extensive lecture transcripts. The human cognitive architecture is constrained by working memory limitations; passive rereading and unstructured highlighting are empirically recognized as among the least effective learning techniques. Cognitive science has consistently proven that active recall and spaced repetition significantly enhance long-term memory retention and conceptual transfer.

1.2 Problem Statement

General-purpose conversational AI models produce unstructured text that cannot be programmatically integrated into diagnostic quizzes, lack spaced repetition scheduling, and often generate superficial recall questions without distractor breakdowns. CogniLearn establishes a dedicated, structured full-stack generative learning suite that bridges document ingestion, curriculum generation, interactive assessment, and algorithmic study scheduling.

2. RELEVANCE OF THE PROJECT

Grounded in Roediger & Karpicke's testing effect and Ebbinghaus' forgetting curve, CogniLearn empowers learners to move from passive consumption to active diagnosis. Instructors can ingest 50-page technical manuals and immediately generate accredited exam items with distractor analyses.
